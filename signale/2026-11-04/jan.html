Robotics from China, Opportunities in DACH

China signals on robotics and humanoids, and what they mean for Germany, Austria and Switzerland — 4 November 2026.

China Signals

medium

1. China ships fewer humanoids in 2026 than planned — prices fall, exports are set to double

What is new: At its humanoid annual conference on 3 Nov 2026, the research firm GGII (高工咨询) cut its forecast: China will ship "超5万台" (over 50,000) humanoids in 2026, down from over 60,000 forecast at the start of the year. The Chinese market grows to over RMB 10 bn; globally it is nearly 60,000 units and nearly US$2 bn. Joint modules became about 30% cheaper in 2026; for 2027 GGII expects further cuts, e.g. large humanoids over 1.6 m from RMB 268,000 to 234,000. More than 15% of humanoid companies will close or be acquired within three years. Overseas shipments are set to double in 2026. GGII names product certification, safety and compliance, data protection and local service as the barriers.

Source: 高工咨询预测：未来三年超15%人形机器人公司将被淘汰 — "GGII forecast: over 15% of humanoid companies will exit within three years" (Yicai via Sina Finance, 3 Nov 2026): finance.sina.com.cn — trade media quoting the institute's figures.

Conformity in the source: no — "product certification" is named in general, no CE directive or standard. Notified body and certificate number: no.

Impact on DACH: Sales — overcapacity and a price war push Chinese makers towards Europe; DACH buyers get better prices. Supply — Chinese joint modules get cheaper; DACH suppliers such as Schaeffler, Wittenstein and Neugart face price pressure. Approval — the barrier GGII itself names is certification; the EU Machinery Regulation applies from 20 Jan 2027. Risk — one maker in six disappears; buyers must secure spare parts and service for years.

Switzerland: CE recognised.

Opportunity for Fastmind: A supplier check for DACH buyers: approval status, financial health, local service. At the same time an offer to Chinese makers who name certification as their own barrier.

Contributes to the strategic question: yes.

medium

2. Nvidia's new humanoid platform from Germany grips with hands from Shenzhen

What is new: According to CNBC on 2 Nov 2026, the Agile One SN platform that Agile Robots presented with Nvidia in Berlin on 21 Oct 2026 uses the "Wuji Hand 2" from the start-up Wuji (team mostly in Shenzhen). Agile Robots has offices in Germany, China and the US. Per Nvidia, research institutions such as ETH Zurich can likely start using the system in early 2027. Nvidia's first reference platform from May uses a body from Unitree (宇树) and hands from Sharpa (manufacturing R&D in Shanghai). The report gives no prices.

Source: "Nvidia's humanoid push relies on China-made robot hands" (CNBC, The China Connection, 2 Nov 2026): cnbc.com — trade media; English-language source, no Chinese title.

Conformity in the source: no. Notified body and certificate number: no.

Impact on DACH: Supply — a German humanoid builder relies on a Chinese hand in its reference system; the hand becomes part of a machine placed on the DACH market. Approval — the system maker carries the CE obligation and needs the supplier's technical documentation for the hand. Risk — a core Chinese component in a US platform makes supply-chain and export-control questions likely.

Switzerland: CE recognised; ETH Zurich is named as a user.

Opportunity for Fastmind: Supplier documentation for Chinese hand makers: technical file and declaration of incorporation, so DACH humanoid builders can take the hand into their CE procedure.

Contributes to the strategic question: yes.

3. Drive modules from China get a distributor abroad — a template for sales in DACH

What is new: In a stock-exchange announcement on 3 Nov 2026, 本末科技 (Hong Kong Stock Exchange, 06731) reported a three-year strategic cooperation with EVERYBOT, a KOSDAQ-listed company from South Korea. EVERYBOT becomes distributor for robots and "动力模组" (drive modules) in Korea: import, sales, marketing, installation and after-sales service. Joint pilot projects in manufacturing, logistics and elderly care are added. The announcement does not say whether the deal is exclusive; Europe is not mentioned.

Source: 本末科技与韩国EVERYBOT合作 推进具身智能产品全球化布局 — "本末科技 partners with Korea's EVERYBOT to expand the global footprint of its embodied-AI products" (Caizhongshe via Sina Finance, 3 Nov 2026, based on the stock-exchange announcement): finance.sina.com.cn — trade media based on a company announcement.

Conformity in the source: no. Notified body and certificate number: no.

Impact on DACH: Sales — Chinese component makers go abroad through a distributor with installation and service, not through their own subsidiary. Approval — for DACH, conformity of the modules belongs in the distributor contract.

Switzerland: CE recognised.

Opportunity for Fastmind: Offer the same model for DACH: Fastmind finds the distributor or integrator and settles approval and service in the contract.

Contributes to the strategic question: yes.

5. The ministry expects over 100,000 humanoids built in 2026

What is new: Next to GGII's figure of over 50,000 shipments stands the forecast of the industry ministry 工信部 MIIT from 19 Oct 2026: over 100,000 units produced in 2026. The figures measure different things (built versus shipped), but together they show a surplus.

Source: State Council Information Office press conference with MIIT (Beijing News / Beike Caijing via Sina Finance, 19 Oct 2026): finance.sina.cn — government statement via media.

Conformity in the source: no. Notified body and certificate number: no.

Impact on DACH: Sales — more supply than demand in China raises export pressure.

Switzerland: CE recognised.

Opportunity for Fastmind: Evidence for the thesis of this issue.

Impact on DACH

Approval: No report names CE, a notified body or a certificate number. GGII itself names certification as an export barrier. For components such as the Wuji hand, the DACH system maker carries the CE obligation and needs the supplier's documentation.

Sales: Chinese makers are under price and volume pressure and want to double exports. They go through distributors and local partners (本末科技 in Korea).

Supply: Chinese joint modules became about 30% cheaper in 2026; Chinese hands already sit in a German humanoid platform.

Risk: Over 15% of Chinese humanoid companies will exit within three years — service, spare parts and warranty must be secured independently of the maker. Chinese core components in US platforms raise export-control questions.

Dates in the next 12 weeks: Humanoid Europe Munich 5–6 Nov 2026 (humanoideurope2026.com) · China International Import Expo Shanghai 5–10 Nov 2026 (k.sina.com.cn) · MEDICA Düsseldorf 16–19 Nov 2026 (ilt.fraunhofer.de) · SPS Nuremberg 24–26 Nov 2026 (uitholland.eu) · Humanoid fair Shanghai 9–11 Dec 2026 (robotexhibition.cn).
