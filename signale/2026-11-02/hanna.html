SKTEC fibres for concrete

New 2. A second European approval route for polymer macro fibres: besides the national abZ, the DIBt lists the European Assessment Document EAD 260067-00-0301 ("Polymer macro fibres reinforced with basalt fibre for the use in concrete", Decision (EU) 2021/1789) and European Technical Assessments for basalt minibars; the abZ for SikaFiber PPM expires on 15 Nov 2026

What is new: The DIBt product-group page lists as technical rules not only the national approvals (22 abZ for polymer fibres, known since 28 Oct) but two European Assessment Documents: EAD 260002-00-0301 for alkali-resistant glass fibres (2016) and EAD 260067-00-0301 for basalt-fibre-reinforced polymer macro fibres (status September 2020, published with Decision (EU) 2021/1789). On that basis European Technical Assessments exist (ETA-20/0599 Basalt MiniBars, ReforceTech, 20 Oct 2020; ETA-19/0246 Cem-FIL MiniBars, 21 Nov 2019). From the abZ list: SikaFiber PPM (Z-3.73-2198) expires on 15 Nov 2026, PM 54/800 (KrampeHarex, Z-3.73-2185) on 24 Mar 2027; latest new decisions Sika Crackstop (6 Mar 2026) and ENNEAFIL Micro-A (20 May 2026).

Why it matters for Hanna (SKTEC): The line so far was: CE to EN 14889-2:2006 for non-structural use, German abZ (18–24 months, via a European holder) for structural use. The EAD shows a third path: for a fibre not covered by EN 14889-2, the manufacturer can obtain an ETA with CE marking from a Technical Assessment Body, valid across Europe. For SKTEC this means checking whether the fibre (polymer, patent US 11,390,965 B2) falls under EN 14889-2 or whether an existing EAD fits or a new one can be requested. An ETA does not automatically replace the German abZ, but it shortens the path into Austria, Switzerland and other EU markets. The expiry of the Sika approval on 15 Nov is a reason to talk to precast plants using that fibre.

Action: Hanna asks the manufacturer: does EN 14889-2 fully cover the SKTEC fibre (raw material, geometry, assessment system)? If not: ask a Technical Assessment Body (DIBt in Germany, OIB in Austria) for a matching EAD. In parallel approach the precast plants whose fibre approval is expiring.

Beite wastewater technology

New 4. The Bergisch-Rheinischer Wasserverband tenders the planning for renewing the aeration of the activated-sludge stage at the Mettmann plant: new fine-bubble floor aerators, "full-area aeration without mixers (where possible)", replacement of the turbo compressors, new Siemens S7 control; bid deadline 11 Nov 2026, 15:00; award 20 % price, 80 % quality

What is new: The BRW (Haan) seeks, in an open procedure, an engineering office for structural design (§ 43 HOAI) and technical equipment design (§ 55 HOAI, equipment groups 1, 3, 4, 7, 8), service phases 1–3, 5–6 and 8, awarded in stages (stage 1 nine months from contract). Goal: fine-bubble floor aeration for intermittent nitrogen removal, without mixers where possible, replacement of the turbo compressors in the blower station, renewal of instrumentation and control, conversion during operation. Award criteria: technical approach 20, organisation 20, references of the responsible person 40, fee 20. Bids binding until 10 Jan 2027; four to ten bidders expected.

Why it matters for Hanna (Beite): This is a tender one stage before the equipment: not the mixer is bought, but the concept that decides whether mixers will be needed at all. The BRW already states the direction: aeration without mixers where possible. For Beite this means two things. First: the market for mixers in the activated-sludge stage may shrink through modern floor aeration; Beite's aerators (QDT series, CE) then become the more relevant product. Second: whoever gets into the planners' product sampling stands in the 2027/28 equipment tender; whoever offers only then competes against the specified make. The BRW, with Angertal and Homberg Süd (known since 28 Oct), is already the most active association in the area.

Action: Hanna identifies the engineering offices bidding for Mettmann (offices with wastewater-plant references in North Rhine-Westphalia) and offers them a Beite data sheet for sampling: deep aeration to 20 m, IE5 motor, 1.5 W/m³ (manufacturer figures), CE declarations QDT/QJB, oxygen transfer to DWA-M 229, references. The goal is not this contract but the planner's product list.

Strategic question

How can manufacturers and organisations from China (robotics, fibres for concrete, wastewater technology) make their product approval for the European market (DACH focus) more efficient and economical?

From this week's sources (own assessment, not legal advice): In wastewater technology it is not the product approval that decides but the planning, years before the equipment tender. The BRW awards the Mettmann planning with an 80 % quality weighting and sets the concept direction ("aeration without mixers"); a Chinese manufacturer saves time and money by entering the engineering offices' product sampling instead of bidding itself (tendigo: Mettmann plant). For fibres in concrete, the more efficient route for a product outside the harmonised standard is a European Technical Assessment via an existing or new assessment document (such as EAD 260067-00-0301), valid across Europe, rather than only the national abZ of 18 to 24 months (DIBt: fibres for concrete). In robotics, conformity becomes a congress theme: Humanoid Europe in Munich discusses on 5–6 Nov the compliance pathways for Chinese robot exports under the amended EU AI Act, with SGS as sponsor and UL Solutions as partner; whoever talks there to the test body and a pilot customer at the same time shortens the path from certification to the first order (humanoidevent.com).
