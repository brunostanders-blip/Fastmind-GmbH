1. Medtronic now sells a Chinese surgical robot itself: USD 700 million for Cornerstone Robotics, Sentire enters the CE markets alongside Hugo

What is new: On 1 September 2026 Medtronic (Galway) announced a strategic partnership with 康诺思腾 Cornerstone Robotics (Hong Kong, development and manufacturing in Shenzhen). The investment of about USD 700 million includes the right to distribute the soft-tissue surgical system Sentire “in select markets outside the U.S. where the system is market approved”, alongside Medtronic's own Hugo RAS. Sentire received its CE mark under the EU MDR on 25 May 2026 for general, gynecologic, thoracic and urologic procedures, plus approval in Singapore (HSA) and China (2024). Medtronic cites Hugo in more than 35 countries with over 50,000 procedures expected by the end of its fiscal year and calls itself “the only company offering this two-platform portfolio”. Cornerstone showed Sentire at the urology congress EAU26 in London in March 2026, has run a clinical evaluation with an NHS trust since 2025 and raised about USD 200 million in November 2025. No release names the European countries Medtronic will serve first. The item is older than two weeks, but it names an approval and a distribution contract and had not been captured before.

Conformity named in the source: yes — CE under EU MDR (Cornerstone names the MDR; Medtronic names the “CE Mark in May 2026”).

Notified body and certificate number named: no.

Consequence for DACH: Distribution — the first Chinese surgical robot arrives not through an importer or a specialist dealer but through the group that was lately the “only economic operator” in Austria (Oberpullendorf clinic, Hugo RAS, known since 31 Oct). Medtronic has sales, service and training in Germany, Austria and Switzerland; the market opens for a Chinese system without building a new structure. Approval — CE under MDR is in place; Medtronic takes on the EU distributor duties under MDR Art. 14, the importer duty under Art. 13 is not named in the releases. Supply — instruments, service and training run through the Medtronic organisation; no room for third parties. Risk — the direct-award justification “no competition for technical reasons” becomes harder for hospital operators when one vendor carries two platforms; for the other Chinese makers (Surgerii, Edge Medical, MicroPort MedBot) access to Medtronic customers is now taken; the country list “select markets” is open, Austria and Germany are not confirmed.

Switzerland: not in the source; Sentire needs Swissmedic, a Swiss authorised representative and swissdamed — Medtronic has its own Swiss entity.

Opportunity for Fastmind: Fastmind cannot offer distribution here; the opportunity is knowledge. Hospital operators in Austria and Germany that re-evaluate their market (Oberpullendorf) learn from Fastmind that Medtronic itself now carries a Chinese platform and that four more CE-MDR systems from China have a dealer or a reference in DACH (Surgerii in Munich, Edge Medical with Dornier MedTech, MicroPort Toumai, Futurtec in orthopaedics). For the makers without a corporate partner, the Medtronic contract is the argument that Chinese surgical robotics is acceptable in Europe — Fastmind brokers hospital access and an importer for them.

Contributes to the strategic question: yes.

Relevance medium

2. A German business delegation led by Hans-Peter Friedrich visits the Beijing hand maker Linkerbot — both sides speak of complementarity, Linkerbot wants to enter Europe through local partners

What is new: On 31 October 2026 a delegation of what the source calls the “德国东部经济国际合作委员会 (IAOW)” — a German business body with an East German focus whose German name the source does not give — led by its chairman Hans-Peter Friedrich (former Federal Minister of the Interior and Vice President of the Bundestag) visited 灵心巧手 Linkerbot in Beijing. Linkerbot showed the O30 hand (brushless motor, worm gear; for precision assembly in automotive and aerospace) and the O6 hand (360 g, grip load up to 50 kg, known since 31 Oct with a 6,666 yuan entry price). The company names three technology paths (linkage, tendon, direct drive), an automated production line in Beijing, in-house micro gearboxes, drive motors and force sensors, and PEEK plastic instead of metal gears. Customers are “leading global manufacturers and top universities”; applications in flexible production, retail and logistics sorting. Both sides noted: Germany is strong in basic research, materials, precision manufacturing, industrial standards and training, China in application and scenarios; competition is shifting “from the form of the whole system and the demo to the upstream supply chain” — core components, cost, reliability. Linkerbot wants to open overseas markets “more through local partners”, test distributed assembly in the Middle East, open its motion-control code and hardware, and “further strengthen its connection with German and European industry”. The release comes from the company and is available as a reprint; Linkerbot's own news page showed no entry at the time of reading.

Source: 德国IAOW代表团到访灵心巧手，探讨机器人产业合作与全球布局 — “German IAOW delegation visits Linkerbot: robotics cooperation and global footprint” (同花顺 AI观察, 31 Oct 2026, reprint of the Linkerbot company release): stock.10jqka.com.cn — trade media · Linkerbot news page (no entry): linkerbot.cn — company · Price anchor Linker Hand O6 and GGII figures (中国经营网, 31 Oct 2026, known since 31 Oct): finance.sina.com.cn — trade media.

Notified body and certificate number named: no.

Consequence for DACH: Supply — in both directions: Linkerbot names materials and precision manufacturing as German strengths and is looking for partners; DACH integrators and humanoid makers get a hand at a tenth of European prices. Distribution — “local partners” means the DACH partner slot for sales, assembly and service is open; Linkerbot plans no European entity of its own. Approval — hands are partly completed machinery (declaration of incorporation, assembly instructions under the Machinery Regulation from 20 Jan 2027), radio modules under RED; the aerospace mention requires an export-control check in both directions. Risk — open hardware and open code make copying and IP disputes easier; price pressure on Schunk, Zimmer, Wittenstein; a politician's visit is not yet a contract.

Switzerland: CE recognised; the same procedure applies to partly completed machinery.

Opportunity for Fastmind: Turn the visit into business: offer Linkerbot a DACH partner model (integrator for assembly and service, conformity documents for the declaration of incorporation, sample procurement with quality inspection in Beijing) and open the component route to Linkerbot for the East German precision manufacturers represented in the delegation — and bring both together on 5–6 November in Munich (Humanoid Europe with supply-and-demand matchmaking).

3. Meta has its household humanoid “Max” built by Fourier in Shanghai — prototype in three months, second generation since September

What is new: According to a report by The Information (31 Oct 2026), picked up by IT之家 and 格隆汇 on 1 Nov, Meta and 傅利叶 Fourier (Shanghai) built a humanoid prototype with the internal name Max in three months: about 1.5 m, 48 kg, Nvidia Thor compute. Mark Zuckerberg sees it as the “physical embodiment” of the Muse AI model for household tasks. Since September both have been working on Max2 with a redesigned torso. No launch date is set and the prototypes will not be sold. Fourier presented the care-focused humanoid GR-3 at CES in January 2026; no source names a European distribution structure for GR-3.

Conformity named in the source: no.

Consequence for DACH: Distribution — Fourier is one of the few Chinese makers with a care-oriented humanoid (GR-3), i.e. a product for hospitals and care in DACH; the Meta work ties up engineering capacity and sends no Europe signal. Supply — the compute comes from Nvidia, not from Chinese chip makers. Approval — household and care humanoids: Machinery Regulation from 20 Jan 2027, RED and EN 18031 for radio, General Product Safety Regulation, EU AI Act for embedded AI; MDR delimitation where there is a medical purpose. Risk — US dependency and export control on compute; Fourier may serve Europe later.

Switzerland: CE recognised.

Opportunity for Fastmind: Keep Fourier as a candidate for a care pilot in DACH with GR-3 and use the Meta reference as a credibility argument; first clarify whether Fourier is looking for a European partner for GR-3.

Consequence for DACH

  • Approval: Another Chinese surgical robot with CE under the MDR stands in Europe — this time with Medtronic as EU distributor; the importer duty is not named in the releases. Robot hands remain partly completed machinery with a declaration of incorporation; household and care humanoids fall under the Machinery Regulation from 20 January 2027, plus RED, the General Product Safety Regulation and the EU AI Act.
  • Distribution: The group that counted as the only vendor in Austria now carries two platforms, one of them from China. Linkerbot explicitly seeks local partners instead of its own European entity. Fourier ties capacity to Meta and sends no Europe signal.
  • Supply: A German delegation and a Chinese hand maker jointly name materials, precision manufacturing and standards as the German contribution; Schaeffler supplies joint modules to two more Chinese customers. The reverse direction — hands from China for DACH integrators — is settled on price.
  • Risk: The direct-award justification “no competition for technical reasons” no longer holds with two platforms from one vendor; the other Chinese surgical makers lose access to Medtronic customers. Open hardware for hands makes copying easier; aerospace as an application requires export control. US compute in Chinese humanoids remains a supply risk.

Content Angle

Strategic Question

In DACH, a politically led business body and a medical technology group are today seeking the Chinese partner: the delegation led by Hans-Peter Friedrich recorded complementarity and cooperation at Linkerbot in Beijing (stock.10jqka.com.cn), and Medtronic — with its own organisation in Germany, Austria and Switzerland — chose a Chinese surgical platform with Cornerstone Robotics (news.medtronic.com). On the Chinese side Linkerbot seeks “local partners” and the “connection with German and European industry” (stock.10jqka.com.cn); the meeting place with explicit supply-and-demand matchmaking is Humanoid Europe on 5–6 November in Munich with Unitree, Galbot, Dobot, Flexiv and Huayan as well as Business Upper Austria and the Humanoid Robotics Association Switzerland (industrieanzeiger.industrie.de).

  • Recommendation: Approach Linkerbot within a week with a DACH partner model and propose a meeting in Munich on 5–6 November — because on 31 October the company publicly sought “local partners” and a connection with German industry, and the delegation visit opened the door but brought no contract.

  • Recommendation: Ask Fourier about a European partner for the care humanoid GR-3 and propose a follow-up project to UKSH Lübeck — because HospiBot documents hospital demand for service humanoids and the Meta reference makes Fourier credible for German operators.
  • Recommendation: Publish the post “Not whether, but through whom” with the three pieces of evidence (Medtronic, Cornerstone CE, Oberpullendorf) — because the Medtronic contract is the strongest evidence yet that Chinese surgical robotics has arrived in Europe, and it has not appeared in any DACH hospital medium so far.
