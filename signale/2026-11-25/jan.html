Robotics from China, opportunities in DACH: 25 November 2026

Signals for Jan from the Chinese robotics and humanoid market.

China signals

1. German trade promotion maps China's robotics market: over 95 % of humanoids from China, but 69 % of uses still research and demo (relevance: medium)
  • What is new: Germany Trade & Invest (GTAI), the federal economic development agency, held the webinar "Humanoid robotics in China" in Hong Kong on 17 Nov 2026. The slides are online.
  • Industrial robots: about 354,000 robots were newly installed in China in 2025 (+20 % on 2024). That is 59 % of global installations; Chinese suppliers hold 55 % and have had the majority since 2024. Source of the figures according to the slide: IFR World Robotics 2026.
  • Humanoids: in the first half of 2026 about 25,000 units were delivered worldwide, more than 19,000 of them to China (78 %). Over 95 % of the recorded units come from Chinese makers. 69 % of the uses are still research, training, demonstration or data collection.
  • Rules in the EU: CE conformity, Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230, EU AI Act, Data Act and a GDPR-compliant transfer mechanism.
  • Rules in China: product standards (GB, GB/T), CCC certification, Chinese Robot Certification, PIPL, Data Security Law and Cybersecurity Law.
  • Rules for exports from China: export control, technology and data transfer and ODI review (examples ABB, SoftBank).
  • Example Neura: the company develops and produces in Metzingen, runs a "NeuraGym" in Hangzhou and has a partnership with Alibaba Cloud. The slide sums it up: "cooperation instead of isolation".
  • Source: "Humanoide Robotik in China: Wie industrielle Fertigung, Lieferketten und staatliche Förderung die Skalierung beschleunigen" (GTAI webinar slides), 17 Nov 2026, gtai.de. Type: primary source, authority.
  • Conformity in the source: yes, CE, Machinery Regulation and EU AI Act appear as one chain; it gives no figures for single products.
  • Notified body and certificate number named: no.
  • Approval: the slides name CE, Machinery Regulation and AI Act as one chain; the Machinery Regulation applies from 20 Jan 2027 (EUR-Lex).
  • Sales: almost all humanoids come from China and most uses are still pilots and research; buyers in DACH are at the start.
  • Supply: Neura shows that a DACH maker finds development and AI partners in China.
  • Risk: export control, data transfer and ODI review apply to technology leaving China and to staff sent there.
  • Switzerland: machines with CE marking are recognised in Switzerland.
  • Opportunity for Fastmind: show DACH buyers and makers the whole chain (CE, Machinery Regulation, AI Act, GDPR, Chinese rules) and use Neura as a reference for the way to China.
  • Contributes to the strategic question: yes.
Already known
  • Beijing action plan embodied AI 2026 to 2028: known since 23.11.
  • New on this: the authority's text confirms the target of 200,000 robots a year in Beijing and more than 60 % local supply in the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei region by 2028 (beijing.gov.cn).
  • MIIT, five humanoid standards: known since 18.11.
  • New on this: the standards SJ/T 12353 to SJ/T 12357 apply from publication; SJ/T 12356 covers electric joints, SJ/T 12357 robot hands (Sina).

Consequence for DACH

  • Approval: CE, Machinery Regulation and AI Act are one chain according to GTAI, not a single certificate; the Machinery Regulation applies from 20 Jan 2027 (gtai.de, EUR-Lex).
  • Sales: Chinese makers deliver over 95 % of humanoids, but 69 % of uses are still research and demo (gtai.de).
  • Supply: Beijing wants more than 60 % of key parts supplied in the region; DACH suppliers therefore need a place in the local supply chain (beijing.gov.cn).
  • Risk: export control, data transfer and ODI review affect technology and staff going to China (gtai.de).
  • Demand in DACH: new tenders for surgical robots or humanoids: sources checked, no new find today.
