Robotics from China, Opportunities in DACH

Signals from the Chinese robotics and humanoid market for Germany, Austria and Switzerland, 9 November 2026.

China signals

Relevance high

1. German suppliers build for Chinese humanoids in China: Freudenberg, Schaeffler, Siemens

  • What is new:
    • Freudenberg: In mid-September Freudenberg opened a robotics co-creation centre in Zhangjiang (Shanghai), focused on seals, lubrication and damping for robots. Freudenberg runs more than 30 plants in China.
    • Schaeffler: In February Schaeffler founded a company for humanoid components in China and names Chinese partners including Leju Robotics and Rokae (珞石). Target by 2035: several thousand humanoids in use and a 10% revenue share. The Leju cooperation has been known since 09.10.; Rokae as a further partner is new.
    • Siemens: At the China International Import Expo (CIIE) Siemens shows solutions for the whole chain of humanoid development; its simulation partner is UBTECH.
  • Source: 采购供货两手抓！欧美巨头扎堆挤进中国机器人产业, "Buying and supplying at once: European and US groups crowd into China's robotics industry" (Shanghai Observer, 9 Nov 2026): k.sina.com.cn, trade media.
  • Conformity in the source: no.
  • Notified body and certificate number given: no.
  • Impact on DACH:
    • Supply: DACH suppliers no longer only sell components to China; they develop them there together with Chinese manufacturers.
    • Sales: Whoever co-develops in China is already set as a supplier for humanoids headed to Europe.
    • Risk: Protection of know-how and export control for components that go into Chinese humanoids.
  • Switzerland: For machinery, CE is recognised in Switzerland.
  • Opportunity for Fastmind: Connect mid-sized DACH suppliers without their own China centre with Chinese humanoid makers, following the Freudenberg and Schaeffler pattern.
  • Contributes to the strategic question: yes.
Relevance medium

2. Deloitte: the next three years decide Chinese robotics in Europe

  • What is new:
    • Report: On 6 Nov 2026 at the CIIE, Deloitte China presented the report 中国具身智能产业出海报告, "Report on the overseas expansion of China's embodied AI industry".
    • Window: According to the report, the next three years are the decisive window for going abroad.
    • Exports: China's industrial robot exports reached USD 1.67 bn in 2025, with a trade surplus for the first time, and USD 1.01 bn in the first half of 2026.
    • Europe: For Europe and Japan the report recommends a strategy of depth rather than breadth; use in production scales first, services in retail and commerce follow.
    • Requirements: Companies need technology, brand, sales channels and compliance at the same time, plus data governance for regional rules.
  • Source:
    • 德勤发布《中国具身智能产业出海报告》, "Deloitte publishes the overseas expansion report" (Yicai, 6 Nov 2026): yicai.com, trade media.
    • Xinhua Finance, 6 Nov 2026: cnfin.com, trade media.
  • Conformity in the source: no; the report speaks of compliance in general, not of CE or the Machinery Regulation.
  • Notified body and certificate number given: no.
  • Impact on DACH:
    • Sales: Chinese manufacturers look for firm partners for sales and service in Europe, not just distributors.
    • Approval: In DACH, "compliance" concretely means CE under the Machinery Regulation from 20 Jan 2027, the Cyber Resilience Act and the GDPR; the report does not name these steps.
  • Switzerland: For machinery, CE is recognised in Switzerland.
  • Opportunity for Fastmind: The gap between "compliance" in the report and the concrete EU obligations is what Fastmind offers.
  • Contributes to the strategic question: yes.
Relevance medium

3. China introduces a mandatory ID for every robot, comments due by 5 Dec 2026

  • What is new:
    • Draft: On 6 Nov 2026 the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) published draft interim rules on a robot product code.
    • Content: Every robot receives its own ID ("一机一码", one device, one code), made up of manufacturer, product description, serial number and a special code; the basis is standard GB/T 48541-2026.
    • Deadline: Comments can be submitted until 5 Dec 2026.
  • Source:
    • 工信部就机器人"身份证"暂行办法征求意见, "MIIT seeks comments on interim rules for the robot ID card" (IT Home, 7 Nov 2026): finance.sina.com.cn, trade media.
    • Reprint 工信部：机器人制造商应当……赋码, "MIIT: robot manufacturers shall assign codes" (7 Nov 2026): k.sina.com.cn, trade media.
    • The MIIT page could not be found.
  • Conformity in the source: no; this is a Chinese rule, not an EU rule.
  • Notified body and certificate number given: no.
  • Impact on DACH:
    • Approval: DACH manufacturers producing robots in China, for components or complete systems, will have to label their devices there.
    • Sales: The ID creates lifecycle traceability; it can serve European buyers as proof of origin and serial number.
    • Risk: Traceability data sits in China; for DACH customers it must be clarified which data this covers.
  • Switzerland: For machinery, CE is recognised in Switzerland; the Chinese ID does not replace CE.
  • Opportunity for Fastmind: Alert DACH companies producing in China to the deadline and align the ID with obligations from CE, the Cyber Resilience Act and the GDPR.
  • Contributes to the strategic question: no.

4. Kärcher, Jungheinrich and Linde Material Handling show robotics at the CIIE

  • What is new:
    • Kärcher: The cleaning robot KIRA B 200 had its Asian premiere at the CIIE. Kärcher board member Bernd Rützler says the quality of Chinese competitors is rising; Kärcher focuses on differentiation.
    • Jungheinrich: At the Jungheinrich stand the manufacturer shows warehouse automation with AI-assisted design and simulation.
    • Linde Material Handling: The KION Group brand is at the CIIE for the ninth time and shows flexible automation for logistics.
  • Source:
    • 机器人竞争加剧 卡赫寻找差异化, "Robot competition intensifies, Kärcher seeks differentiation" (Zhongjing, 8 Nov 2026): finance.sina.com.cn, trade media.
    • KIRA B 200: finance.sina.com.cn, trade media.
    • Jungheinrich: 在进博会上感受AI产业新脉动, "Feeling the new pulse of the AI industry at the CIIE" (Xinhua, 8 Nov 2026): k.sina.com.cn, trade media.
    • Linde Material Handling: AGV Network, 8 Nov 2026: k.sina.com.cn, trade media.
  • Conformity in the source: no.
  • Notified body and certificate number given: no.
  • Impact on DACH:
    • Sales: DACH makers of service and logistics robotics sell in China against strong local competition.
    • Supply: The same competition is a source of components and manufacturing for DACH makers.
  • Switzerland: For machinery, CE is recognised in Switzerland.
  • Opportunity for Fastmind: Check sources in China for DACH makers of cleaning and logistics robotics; the reverse direction DACH to China runs through the same contacts.
  • Contributes to the strategic question: yes.

Impact on DACH

  • Components: The route from DACH to China is open; Freudenberg and Schaeffler develop in China with Chinese humanoid makers (Shanghai Observer).
  • Sales: Chinese manufacturers want to go deep in Europe and need partners for compliance and sales (Yicai).
  • Approval: China is introducing a mandatory ID per robot; in the EU the Machinery Regulation applies from 20 Jan 2027. Manufacturers producing in China need both (IT Home).
  • Competition: Kärcher sees the quality of Chinese competitors rising (Zhongjing).
  • Demand in DACH: New tenders for surgical robots or humanoids in DE, AT and CH since 30 Oct 2026: sources checked, no new find today (TED).

Content angle

  • Thesis: The humanoids come from China, but seals, bearings and simulation come from German firms that co-develop them in China.
  • Evidence: Freudenberg with a co-creation centre in Shanghai, Schaeffler with Leju and Rokae, Siemens with UBTECH (Shanghai Observer).

Strategic question

  • DACH seeks China: Freudenberg, Schaeffler and Siemens work in China with Chinese robotics makers such as Leju, Rokae and UBTECH (Shanghai Observer).
  • DACH shows up in China: Kärcher, Jungheinrich and Linde Material Handling exhibit their robotics at the CIIE (Zhongjing, Xinhua).
  • China seeks DACH: According to Deloitte, Chinese embodied AI firms want to enter Europe within the next three years and need local compliance and sales for it (Yicai).
