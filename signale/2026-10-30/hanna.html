SKTEC fibres for concrete

2. New EU Construction Products Regulation (EU) 2024/3110 applicable since 8 Jan 2026: fibres under EN 14889-2 stay on the old route because the harmonised standard was made under Regulation 305/2011; the standard itself has been unchanged since 2006 (DIBt notice 16 Feb 2026; DIN standards status)

What is new: The German Institute for Construction Technology (DIBt) explains the transition: the “new” procedures of the 2024 regulation apply only once a product is covered by a harmonised standard or European Assessment Document made under the new regulation. For products whose standard was still made under the 2011 regulation, the declaration of performance, CE marking and assessment of constancy of performance continue under the old rules; the new environmental requirements do not yet apply to them; the digital product passport comes only with a delegated act plus 18 months of transition. For polymer fibres the harmonised standard is EN 14889-2:2006; a revision draft (prEN 14889-2:2018, CEN/TC 104/WG 11) was withdrawn, the 2006 edition remains current.

Why it matters for Hanna (SKTEC): The CE route for SKTEC fibres is predictable for the coming years: declaration of performance to EN 14889-2:2006 with a notified body (system 1 for structural, system 3 for non-structural use), no new EU procedure, no product passport before the delegated act. What CE does not replace stays as described on 28 Oct: structural use in Germany requires the national technical approval (DIBt list with 22 approvals, known since 28 Oct). Chinese suppliers advertising “CE to EN 14889-2:2006” (known since 28 Oct) are formally on the right document level, because there is no newer one.

Action: For the SKTEC meeting, prepare a two-column overview “What CE delivers today / what CE does not deliver”: left the declaration of performance to EN 14889-2:2006 for phase 1 (precast, industrial floors without structural fibre action), right the DIBt application for phase 2. Check for Hanna: which notified body issued SKTEC's CE and for which assessment system?

Beite wastewater technology

4. Tender Neckarsulm wastewater plant, fourth treatment stage (200,000 PE): process equipment with 8 submersible mixers (IE4, variable frequency drives), EU-wide, price only, bid deadline 9 Nov 2026 (notice 5 Oct, version 29 Oct 2026)

What is new: The Abwasserzweckverband Unteres Sulmtal (central procurement office of the City of Neckarsulm) awards, in an EU-wide open procedure without lots and without variant bids, the supply, installation and commissioning of the mechanical and electrical equipment for the fourth treatment stage (Ulm process: powdered activated carbon, SBR reactors, cloth filters), with optional maintenance for the first four operating years. In scope: 8 submersible mixers (IE4, with VFD, lifting gear, guide sleeves) for the SBR reactors, 3 inlet pumps, 4 disc filters (at least 360 m² in total), 4 decanters, dosing systems with compressed-air station, stainless steel piping DN 50 to 800, Siemens S7-1500 control. Award criterion: price. Eligibility under § 6e EU VOB/A. Execution 11 Jan 2027 to 15 Dec 2028, commissioning 4 to 15 Dec 2028. The State of Baden-Württemberg funds the project with about EUR 5.7 million.

Why it matters for Hanna (Beite): After Cham (6 hyperboloid mixers, deadline 18 Nov, known since 28 Oct) this is the second award within three weeks that specifies mixers, and the first EU-wide one: bidders from the whole single market may bid, and the award is decided on price alone. Submersible mixers correspond to Beite's CE-certified QJB series. Beite's route is not its own bid but the component in the bid of a German process-equipment bidder; eligibility (references, prequalification) then rests with that bidder.

Action: Hanna approaches the process-equipment suppliers bidding on Neckarsulm and Cham (plant builders with cloth-filter and SBR references) with a component offer for the mixers: IE4/IE5 evidence, CE declaration QJB, price delivered to site, spare-parts and service commitment for the maintenance option. The 9 Nov deadline is too short for a first offer; the target is the same bidder's next procurement cycle.

Regulatory and compliance update

  • CE / Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230: application from 20 Jan 2027 unchanged; a list of harmonised standards for the Machinery Regulation is still not published in the Official Journal (known since 2 Oct), the list for the old Machinery Directive was last updated on 7 Sep 2026; the Commission guide exists only as a draft (IBF: status of harmonised standards). Trade defence: robotic lawn mower case ahead of the decision due by 19 Jan 2027 (signal 1, status).
  • CE / construction products: Regulation (EU) 2024/3110 applicable since 8 Jan 2026, transition for products under legacy standards such as EN 14889-2:2006 (signal 2).
  • EU MDR: the targeted revision of MDR and IVDR proposed in December 2025 (fewer unannounced audits, abolition of recertification after five years, fee discounts of 50 % for micro and 25 % for small enterprises at notified bodies) passed the Parliament's committees in September 2026; plenary in Q1 2027, then trilogue, adoption expected summer 2027. The TÜV association warns of weakened oversight. Until then the MDR applies unchanged, also to surgical and care robotics (Emergo by UL, 9 Oct 2026 · Deutsches Ärzteblatt · Noerr: the Commission's reform proposal).
  • REACH: the Commission officially set aside the full revision of REACH in April 2026; a registration duty for polymers is therefore off the table for now, work concentrates on simplification via comitology and better enforcement. The universal PFAS restriction remains open (decision phase end of 2026, known since 7 Sep); the PFHxA ban for textiles applies since 10 Oct 2026 (known since 7 Sep) (National Law Review, 28 Apr 2026 · REACH24H · Council of the EU, document ST 10294/2026).
  • LkSG / CSDDD: the government bill replacing the German Supply Chain Act with an act on international corporate responsibility (Bundestag printed paper 21/2474, CSDDD 1:1) had its first reading in January 2026 and is not adopted; reports of a limitation of scope to companies with 5,000 or more employees “in autumn 2026” rest on secondary sources and contradict each other (known since 7 Sep) (German Bundestag, sitting week 3/2026 · printed paper 21/2474).
  • EU AI Act: deadlines for high-risk AI in machinery postponed to 2 Aug 2028 (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, known since 11 Sep); nothing new.

Open strategic question

How can manufacturers from China make their product approval for the European market (DACH focus) more efficient?

A new example from October: no new find today. The most recently documented cases remain the TÜV Rheinland certificates for Chinese robotics makers at IFA (known since 14 Sep) and the humanoid that passed the EN 18031 cybersecurity test (known since 18 Sep).

Structurally, this week's signals allow the following (own assessment, not legal advice):

  • Wastewater technology: in Neckarsulm and at the Ruhrverband no product approval is examined, but bidder eligibility (prequalification, form 124, references) and price. The most efficient route for a Chinese manufacturer is therefore not its own bid, but the CE-compliant component in the bid of an established German bidder.
  • Fibres for concrete: the Construction Products Regulation transition freezes the CE route for polymer fibres at EN 14889-2:2006. A Chinese manufacturer needs no new EU procedure, but a notified body and, for structural use in Germany, a European approval holder.
  • Robotics: approval does not end at CE. The robotic lawn mower case shows that trade defence (anti-dumping, retroactive duties) runs in parallel to conformity; anyone shipping to the EU in 2027 plans customs and origin questions as well.
