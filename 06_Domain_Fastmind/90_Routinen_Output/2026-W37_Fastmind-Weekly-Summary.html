A-2 · Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 — 128 days, no transition period

Two hard dates in range: 20.10.2026 (member states must have notified national penalty regimes, Art. 50(1)) and 20.01.2027 (full application, Directive 2006/42/EC repealed, no transition and no sell-off period). Safety-relevant software now counts as a safety component; cybersecurity tightened; machines with AI showing "self-evolving behaviour" require a notified body — self-declaration is gone. Importers, online distributors and authorised representatives are named as duty-holders in their own right.

Why it matters: turns China market access from a sales question into a legal one, cutting both ways at once. The real constraint is notified-body capacity, not documentation.

A-3 · EmpCo (EU) 2024/825 applies 27.09.2026 — the textile deadline everyone missed

A self-correction by Partner A. Earlier runs recorded that the fibre side "has no deadline". Wrong: the first binding textile deadline comes from the Empowering Consumers Directive, applying from 27 September 2026. Banned: generic environmental claims without proof, non-certified sustainability labels, offset-based climate-neutrality claims, and whole-product claims true only of parts. No transition, existing stock included.

Caveat that must be respected: EmpCo is B2C consumer protection. A fibre supplier is affected indirectly, via the brand customer who must substantiate claims and pushes evidence down the chain. Presenting it as a direct compliance duty would be factually wrong.

6 · Market Opportunity Assessment

DACH robotics

Stock is high, investment is not. Density 449/10,000 (rank 3), stock 278,900 units (+4 %), ~40 % of all EU factory robots — but VDMA expects −5 % revenue to €14.1bn in 2026 after −7 %, and German cobot deliveries −0.3 % while the world market grows ~17 % p.a. Orders January 2026 real −6 %; four-month split domestic −4 %, export +2 %. Installations nonetheless +7 % to 28,355 with 42 % automotive — the growth that exists is concentrated where Fastmind is not positioned. Compliance cost drivers: notified-body capacity before 20.01.2027, German-language ProdSG documentation, CE/RED approval, telemetry/GDPR evidence.

DACH education & talent

~133,900 unfillable MINT posts, ~58 % at skilled-worker level; replacement demand rising to >70,000 academics and ~295,000 skilled workers per year within five years. Recognition permits extended to 24–36 months (window widens), Work-and-Stay-Agentur stalled (bottleneck stays organisational), visa rejection data classified (risk becomes unquantifiable). Willingness to engage with China-sourced talent is bifurcated: business is willing and localising (93 % of 627 AHK-surveyed firms stay engaged) while the education system retreats (German students in China −79 %; 0.1 % of pupils learning Chinese). The opportunity sits in the gap — and in the near-empty counter-direction Germany → China.

Sino-European dynamics

Two movements run against each other and need the same competence. Chinese manufacturers build European legal and industrial substance (Estun's Polish plant live October 2026; Inovance's German GmbH; AgiBot from Munich) while German manufacturers sell better abroad than at home. European vendors have converted certification into an explicit anti-China sales argument, yet a European notified body is publicly certifying Chinese robotics at a European consumer fair — the compliance route is being institutionalised, not closed. On the talent side the political temperature rises quietly: research-visa rejection rates are classified, and Research Security has moved from security debate into university administration.

Risk factors

  • Notified-body capacity before 20.01.2027 — the binding constraint, and it cannot be compressed.
  • DACH demand weakness — a second consecutive down year means budget conservatism in the exact buyer group.
  • Competitor pre-emption on compliance — European vendors already own the certification argument.
  • Regulatory relief misread as relief — the AI Act delay and the weakened CSDDD/LkSG remove two arguments Fastmind might have used.
  • Political friction on the talent side — classified visa data plus Research Security scrutiny raise the proof burden.
  • Evidence fragility — six unresolved data conflicts; any used unchecked is a credibility risk.
